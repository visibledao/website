Where the money goes

All revenue comes in first. Then each active expense draws what it needs, on its own. Every coin remembers where it came from.

The third entry

Every movement debits one stock and credits another. Each receipt is a SHA-256 hash of its entry plus the previous receipt, so changing any past entry breaks every receipt after it. These receipts are simulated and stay in your browser. On-chain, they would be Bitcom transactions anyone can audit.

Questions this model forces

  • Who can propose an expense, and what moves it from proposed to active? Here it's one dropdown. In practice, it could be a member vote at the monthly close.
  • What happens to money given for an expense that isn't approved, or is discontinued? Here it waits in the general fund, still restricted, until that expense is active again or the giver releases it.
  • Should surplus roll over as a reserve, or return to the general fund? A reserve smooths bad months. Returning it keeps money free for whatever is needed next.
  • Which coin pays first? This model spends restricted money first, then oldest. Change that rule and the "paid by" story changes, even though the totals don't.
  • Who sets priority? Order only matters when unrestricted money runs short. It's a governance decision that members could revisit at each monthly close.
  • What would make these receipts real? Each ledger entry could be posted as a MAP-tagged Bitcom record, with app=visibledao, from a DAO-owned address.
